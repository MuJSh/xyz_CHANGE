From September 6 to 11, 2026, the biennial iCACGP-IGAC 2026 International Atmospheric Chemistry Conference was held in Heraklion, Crete, Greece. The conference was jointly organized by the International Commission on Atmospheric Chemistry and Global Pollution (iCACGP) and the International Global Atmospheric Chemistry (IGAC) Project. This year's conference received more than 900 abstract submissions from the global atmospheric chemistry community. Following peer review by the conference committee, 65 oral presentations and 546 poster presentations were selected. Nearly 800 international scholars attended the meeting, which highlighted recent advances across atmospheric chemistry.

Bin Luo, a PhD student from Shandong University, had his submitted abstract selected for an oral presentation. He delivered a talk titled "Dominant role of Southeast Asian emissions in anthropogenic increases in global tropospheric ozone and southward tropical growth, 2000-2019". The presentation introduced the group's latest research on the role of anthropogenic emission changes in driving global tropospheric ozone changes.

Using global atmospheric chemistry model simulations, the study quantitatively assessed the effects of anthropogenic emission changes from 2000 to 2019 on the global tropospheric ozone burden and ozone radiative forcing. It further quantified the regional contributions of anthropogenic emission changes in East Asia, South Asia, and Southeast Asia to changes in the global tropospheric ozone burden. This work was jointly supervised by Prof. Yuqiang Zhang and Prof. Likun Xue, with support from the National Natural Science Foundation of China, the Taishan Scholars Program of Shandong Province, and related research programs.

iCACGP and IGAC have long promoted the development of global atmospheric chemistry research and international scientific collaboration. Their joint international conference provides an important platform for academic exchange on global issues related to air pollution, climate change, ecosystems, and human health. The meeting brings together researchers from around the world to discuss research frontiers and future directions in atmospheric chemistry.
