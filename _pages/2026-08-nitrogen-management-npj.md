An international collaboration between our group at Shandong University and colleagues from Duke University, Columbia University, and IIASA published a study in *npj Climate and Atmospheric Science*: "Efficient nitrogen management as a win-win strategy for air quality, health, and climate".

In this study, we applied two global climate models, CESM and GISS, to investigate how reactive nitrogen (Nr) management policies affect global air quality and climate. By mid-century, both models simulate positive aerosol radiative forcing (RF) and negative ozone RF, yielding a net positive RF of 150 mW m<sup>-2</sup> (95% CI: 120-180 mW m<sup>-2</sup>) in GISS and 60 mW m<sup>-2</sup> (10-120 mW m<sup>-2</sup>) in CESM.

Global air quality also improves, with global area-weighted mean PM<sub>2.5</sub> concentrations decreasing by 0.16 ug m<sup>-3</sup> (0.07-0.25 ug m<sup>-3</sup>) and 0.13 ug m<sup>-3</sup> (0.05-0.21 ug m<sup>-3</sup>), and global area-weighted mean maximum daily 8-h average ozone decreasing by 1.69 ppb (1.48-1.89 ppb) and 0.99 ppb (0.88-1.10 ppb) in GISS and CESM, respectively. Regional PM<sub>2.5</sub> reductions exceed 5 ug m<sup>-3</sup>. Moreover, more than 80% of avoided premature deaths occur across 0-60 degrees N.
